Real observation · radius not measured

The drawing specifies an R0.8 mm inside radius; the available punch has a nominal R0.5 mm radius. The part shows a fit discrepancy, but the photograph alone cannot establish a numeric value for the actual inside radius.

What the photograph demonstrates

The photograph is visual evidence of the observed fit issue. It is not a metrological measurement: perspective, framing and the absence of a dimensional reference prevent a reliable measurement of the inside radius or deviation from the drawing.

The fit problem is observed; the actual radius value remains to be verified.

Why punch radius is not enough

In air bending, the final radius is not determined by the punch's nominal radius alone. Material and mechanical properties, actual thickness, die opening and geometry, and process conditions also influence the result. Comparing the R0.8 drawing callout with an R0.5 punch is a useful clue for investigation, not proof of the formed radius.

This is why Grievesk Bend starts by estimating the formed inside radius and only then derives a K-factor consistent with that geometry. When a measurement is missing, we do not present a number as though it were an experimental result.

What is still needed for verification

  • Exact steel grade and any available mechanical properties.
  • Drawing tolerance for the R0.8 radius.
  • Direct measurement of the inside radius or a verifiable cross-section.
  • If possible, a measurement of the fit deviation.

Once these details are available, the case can move from a qualitative observation to a quantitative comparison between nominal geometry and the manufactured part.
