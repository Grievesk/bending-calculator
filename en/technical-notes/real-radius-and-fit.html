Case 01 · Inside radius and fit

When the formed radius affects fit

The inside radius influences component geometry. When two parts must fit together, a small difference can become visible during assembly.

What to look for

CAD defines a nominal inside radius, but the formed radius also depends on material, actual thickness, die opening and geometry, punch, target angle and bending process. The tool's nominal radius is not necessarily the final sheet-metal radius.

If the flat-pattern model assumes an unrepresentative radius, even a mathematically consistent K-factor can produce a less accurate prediction of the final geometry. Grievesk Bend therefore aims to estimate the formed radius first and derive a consistent K-factor afterwards.

Photographic documentation: the mating-edge detail will be included in the illustrated version of this case; dimensional conclusions still require comparable measurements.

What can — and cannot — be concluded

The photograph shows the bend and component edge, but it cannot by itself establish the exact inside radius, actual thickness or deviation from CAD. Quantitative verification requires comparable dimensions or measurements and the bending conditions.

This case is therefore a starting point for data collection, not an unsupported numerical conclusion. When measurements and process parameters are available, the predicted radius can be compared with the part and the effect on fit assessed.

Useful data to complete the case

  • Sheet thickness and material.
  • CAD inside radius and intended tolerance.
  • Punch, V-opening and final angle.
  • Measured inside radius or a verifiable section.
  • Description of the fit issue.

We will not assign values that have not been measured. The collection will grow with traceable real-world observations, keeping direct measurements, CAD/CAM values and estimates distinct.

The Grievesk Bend principle

Estimate the formed inside radius first, derive a K-factor consistent with the geometry, then verify the resulting development. Real cases help reveal where the model works and where it needs improvement.
