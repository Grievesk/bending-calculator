How to measure the flanges

Dimension A/B is parallel to its respective leg. Extension lines are perpendicular to the leg and reach the projection of the bend outer envelope. Each dimension includes the straight section plus outside setback (OSS); at 90°, OSS = Ri + s.

Dimension convention: dimension line A/B is parallel to the leg; extension lines are perpendicular to it and end at the projected outer bend envelope. The formula uses OSS = (Ri + s) × tan(bend angle / 2), equal to Ri + s for a 90° bend.

Enter the inside angle: 0° = flanges touching, 45° = acute, 90° = right, 135° = obtuse. A ± sign used by some CAM systems indicates bend direction; it does not change the inside-angle magnitude.

Before using a calculated value in production

The sample tool derives K from the dimensions you enter. The development calculator uses an indicative model, not a calibrated result for every material, tool and machine. Check tooling load limits and manufacturer data, then validate the setup with a test bend before production. Values from the model are not a substitute for measured shop-floor data.

Technical Guide: Bending Mechanics and Flat-Pattern Calculation

1. Air Bending vs Bottoming/Coining vs Rotating Dies (WingBend)

In traditional air bending, the sheet contacts the tooling at only three points: the punch tip and the two shoulders of the V-die. As a result, the inside radius (ri) is not determined by the punch radius (unless the punch radius is larger than the natural radius); instead, it forms freely in proportion to the V-die opening:

  • Carbon steel: ri ≈ 0.16 × V (about V/6)
  • Stainless steel: ri ≈ 0.20 × V (about V/5, due to greater springback)
  • Soft/medium aluminium: ri ≈ 0.14 × V (about V/7)

In tangential rotating-wing dies (such as WingBend or Rolla-V), the die shoulders are replaced by semi-round inserts that rotate in sync with the descending punch. This reduces the leverage needed for the minimum flange (down to 0.45 × V), helps prevent cosmetic marks (ideal for brushed stainless steel or pre-painted sheet), and makes the sheet closely follow the punch radius (ri ≈ Rp).

2. Why CAD Flat Patterns Can Be Wrong (Especially for Multi-Bend Parts)

Many designers create 3D models using their software's default parameters (e.g. ri = 1.0 mm and K = 0.33 or 0.44). On the shop floor, the operator selects the die based on material thickness; for example, a 3 mm sheet may be bent using a V = 24 mm opening.

With a 24 mm V-die opening, the actual inside radius may be about 3.8–4.0 mm, not 1.0 mm. A larger radius changes the bend deduction significantly. If the error is 0.5 mm per bend, a box or omega profile with four bends can accumulate an error exceeding 2.0 mm, potentially making the part unusable.

Indicative air-bending force: F [kN] ≈ 1.42 × Rm [MPa] × L [m] × s² [mm²] / V [mm]. This is an estimate for air bending only; do not use it alone to size a press brake or tooling.
Bend Allowance (BA): π × (β / 180) × (ri + K × s)
Outside Setback (OSS): tan(β / 2) × (ri + s)
Bend Deduction (BD): 2 × OSS − BA  (where β is the bend angle)

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

When can a punch that is too sharp cause a scoring effect?

The punch-radius-to-thickness ratio is a practical indicator, not a universal threshold. A very sharp punch can concentrate deformation and mark the inside surface; risk also depends on material, ductility, angle, tooling clearance and bending method. Treat Rp < 0.63 × s as a rule of thumb to investigate, not a physical limit that applies to every material.

How does the rolling direction affect bending?

Rolling direction can affect ductility and cracking risk, particularly with tight inside radii and high-strength materials. In many cases, orienting the bend line perpendicular to the rolling direction reduces cracking risk compared with a parallel bend, but behaviour depends on alloy, temper, thickness, radius and material specifications. It is not safe to assume that one orientation always requires more force or always produces less springback; check material guidance and test when cracking risk matters.

What is the difference between Bend Allowance (BA) and Bend Deduction (BD)?

The Bend Allowance (BA) is the arc length along the neutral axis within the bend region. The Bend Deduction (BD) is subtracted from the sum of outside dimensions measured to theoretical sharp corners to obtain the flat pattern: for a profile with N bends, flat length = sum of outside dimensions − sum of BD values. BA and BD are not interchangeable and depend on geometry, angle, inside radius and the selected K-factor.
